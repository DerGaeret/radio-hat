## 1. Zusammenfassung

### 1.1 Überblick

Synchroner Abwärtswandler 4,5–28 V auf einstellbare Ausgangsspannung, 3 A Dauerstrom, integrierte MOSFETs (High-Side 85 mΩ, Low-Side 40 mΩ), feste Schaltfrequenz 350 kHz, Peak-Current-Mode, interne Kompensation, interner Softstart 5 ms, FCCM (erzwungener kontinuierlicher Betrieb: auch ohne Last keine Pulsauslassung, Frequenz bleibt konstant; laut TI für Audio gedacht, damit keine hörbaren Frequenzen entstehen), Überstromschutz beider FETs mit Hiccup, Überspannungsschutz am Ausgang, Übertemperaturabschaltung. Gehäuse DDC (SOT-23-THN, 6 Pins), 2,9 × 2,8 mm. Bestellnummern TPS54308DDCR (3000/Rolle), TPS54308DDCT (250), Aufdruck „4308“, MSL 1 / 260 °C.

### 1.4 Formeln (Kap. 6.3.5, 7.2.3)

| Gl. | Formel | Zweck |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | R4 = (V_START · V_ENfalling/V_ENrising − V_STOP) / (Ip · (1 − V_ENfalling/V_ENrising) + Ih) | oberer UVLO-Widerstand (VIN → EN); Ip 0,7 µA, Ih 1,55 µA, V_ENfalling 1,19 V, V_ENrising 1,22 V |
| 2 | R5 = R4 · V_ENfalling / (V_STOP − V_ENfalling + R4 · (Ip + Ih)) | unterer UVLO-Widerstand (EN → GND); Hysterese > 500 mV empfohlen |
| 3, 7 | VOUT = VREF · (R2/R3 + 1) | Ausgangsspannung; R2 (oben) etwa 100 kΩ, Widerstände 1 % |
| 6 | R3 = R2 · VREF / (VOUT − VREF) | unterer Teilerwiderstand |
| 4 | ΔVIN = IOUT(max) · 0,25 / (C_BULK · fsw) + IOUT(max) · ESR_max | Eingangswelligkeit |
| 5 | I_CIN(RMS) = IOUT(max) / 2 | Effektivstrom im Eingangskondensator |
| 8 | L_MIN = VOUT · (VIN(max) − VOUT) / (VIN(max) · K_IND · IOUT · fsw) | Mindestinduktivität; K_IND = Welligkeit relativ zum Höchststrom, 0,2 bei höherem ESR, höher bei Keramik; Beispiel 0,3 |
| 9 | I_L(RMS) = √(IOUT(max)² + 1/12 · (VOUT · (VIN(max) − VOUT) / (VIN(max) · L · fsw · 0,8))²) | Effektivstrom der Spule |
| 10 | I_L(PK) = IOUT(max) + VOUT · (VIN(max) − VOUT) / (1,6 · VIN(max) · L · fsw) | Spitzenstrom der Spule (Sättigung beachten) |
| 11 | C_O > 2 · ΔIOUT / (fsw · ΔVOUT) | Ausgangskapazität für Lastsprünge (zwei Takte Reaktionszeit) |
| 12 | C_O > 1/(8 · fsw) · I_ripple / V_OUTripple | Ausgangskapazität für die Welligkeit |
| 13 | R_ESR < V_OUTripple / I_ripple | höchster ESR |
| 14 | f_o = 5,1 / (VOUT · C_O) | Durchtrittsfrequenz ohne Vorkopplung, soll < 40 kHz |
| 15 | I_COUT(RMS) = 1/√12 · VOUT · (VIN(max) − VOUT) / (VIN(max) · L · fsw · N_C) | Effektivstrom je Ausgangskondensator |
| 16 | C6 = 1 / (2π · f_o · R2) | Vorkopplungskondensator parallel zum oberen Widerstand |

### 1.5 Bauteilwahl und Versorgung (7.2.3, 7.3)

- Eingang: Keramik > 10 µF als Entkopplung, 0,1 µF zusätzlich optional, Spannungsfestigkeit über VIN(max); bei entfernter Quelle zusätzlich etwa 47 µF Elko.
- BOOT: 0,1 µF Keramik X7R/X5R.
- Ausgang: Keramik mit kleinem ESR; Alterung, Temperatur und Gleichspannungsverlust einrechnen; Beispiel 2 × 22 µF 25 V X7R.
- Spule: Effektiv- und Sättigungsstrom nach Gl. 9 und 10 einhalten.
- UVLO-Beispiel der Referenzschaltung: R4 511 k, R5 105 k → Start 6,74 V, Stopp 5,83 V.
- Die Eingangsspannung muss gut geregelt sein.

### 1.6 Layout (7.4)

VIN- und GND-Flächen breit; Eingangs- und Ausgangskondensator so nah wie möglich am Baustein, mit genügend Vias; SW-Leitung kurz und breit; kein Schaltstrom unter dem Baustein; eigene VOUT-Leitung zum oberen Teilerwiderstand; Kelvin-Anschluss der Rückführung an den GND-Pin; Rückführung weg vom Schaltknoten, möglichst mit Masseschirm; FB-Knoten klein; GND zwischen Ausgangskondensator und GND-Pin breit. Land Pattern: 6 Pads 1,1 × 0,6 mm, Raster 0,95 mm, Padmitten 2,7 mm auseinander, Lötstopp 0,07 mm um das Pad.

### 6 Detailed Description

#### 6.1 Overview

```text
The device is a 28V, 3A, synchronous step-down (buck) converter with two integrated N-channel MOSFETs. To
improve performance during line and load transients, the device implements a constant-frequency, peak current
mode control which reduces output capacitance. The optimized internal compensation network minimizes the
external component counts and simplifies the control loop design.
The switching frequency of the TPS54308 is fixed to 350kHz. This device operates in continuous conduction
mode (FCCM) during light load conditions. During FCCM, the switching frequency is maintained at an almost
constant level over the entire load range, which is desired for applications requiring tight control of the switching
frequency and output voltage ripple at the cost of lower efficiency under light load.
The TPS54308 starts switching when VIN is equal to 4.5V. The operating current is 300μA (typical) when the
device is not switching and under no load. When the device is disabled, the supply current is 2µA (typical).
The integrated 85mΩ high-side MOSFET and 40mΩ low-side MOSFET allow for high-efficiency power supply
designs with continuous output currents up to 3A.
The TPS54308 reduces the external component count by integrating the boot recharge diode. The bias voltage
for the integrated high-side MOSFET is supplied by an external capacitor on the BOOT to PH pins. The boot
capacitor voltage is monitored by an UVLO circuit and turns the high-side MOSFET off when the voltage falls
below a preset threshold of 2.1V (typical).
The device minimizes excessive output overvoltage transients by taking advantage of the overvoltage
comparator. When the regulated output voltage is greater than 118% of the nominal voltage, the overvoltage
comparator is activated, and the high-side MOSFET is turned off and masked from turning on until the output
voltage is lower than 104%.
The TPS54308 device has internal 5ms soft-start time to minimize inrush currents.
```

##### 6.3.1 Fixed-Frequency PWM Control

```text
The device uses a fixed-frequency, peak current-mode control. The output voltage is compared through external
resistors on the FB pin to an internal voltage reference by an error amplifier. An internal oscillator initiates the
turn-on of the high-side power switch. The error amplifier output is compared to the current of the high-side
power switch. When the power-switch current reaches the error amplifier output voltage level, the high-side
power switch is turned off and the low-side power switch is turned on. The error amplifier output voltage
increases and decreases as the output current increases and decreases. The device implements a current limit
by clamping the error amplifier voltage to a maximum level. The device also implements a minimum clamp for
improved transient-response performance.
```

##### 6.3.2 Force Continuous Conduction Mode (FCCM)

```text
The TPS54308 is designed to operate in forced continuous conduction mode (FCCM) during light load
conditions. During FCCM, the switching frequency is maintained at an almost constant level over the entire
load range, which is desired for applications requiring tight control of the switching frequency and output voltage
ripple at the cost of lower efficiency under light load. For some audio applications, this mode can help avoid
switching frequency drop into an audible range that can introduce some "noise".
```

##### 6.3.3 Error Amplifier

```text
The device has a transconductance amplifier as the error amplifier. The error amplifier compares the FB voltage
to the lower of the internal soft-start voltage or the internal 0.596V voltage reference. The transconductance of
the error amplifier is 240 µA/V typically. The frequency compensation components are placed internally between
the output of the error amplifier and ground.
```

##### 6.3.4 Slope Compensation and Output Current

```text
The device adds a compensating ramp to the signal of the switch current. This slope compensation prevents
subharmonic oscillations as the duty cycle increases. The available peak inductor current remains constant over
the full duty-cycle range.
```

```text
The EN pin has an internal pullup current source which allows the user to float the EN pin to enable the device.
```

##### 6.3.11 Overcurrent Protection

```text
The device is protected from overcurrent conditions by cycle-by-cycle current limiting on both the high-side
MOSFET and the low-side MOSFET.
```

###### 6.3.11.1 High-Side MOSFET Overcurrent Protection

```text
The device implements current mode control, which uses the internal COMP voltage to control the turn-off of the
high-side MOSFET and the turn-on of the low-side MOSFET on a cycle-by-cycle basis. During each cycle, the
switch current and the current reference generated by the internal COMP voltage are compared. When the peak
switch current intersects the current reference, the high-side switch turns off.
```

###### 6.3.11.2 Low-Side MOSFET Overcurrent Protection

```text
While the low-side MOSFET is turned on, the conduction current is monitored by the internal circuitry. During
normal operation, the low-side MOSFET sources current to the load. At the end of every clock cycle, the
low-side MOSFET sourcing current is compared to the internally set low-side sourcing current-limit. When the
inductor valley current is exceeded by the low-side source current limit, the high-side MOSFET does not turn on
and the low-side MOSFET stays on for the next cycle. The high-side MOSFET turns on again when the inductor
valley current is below the low-side sourcing current-limit at the start of a cycle as shown in Figure 6-2.
Furthermore, if an output overload condition occurs for more than the hiccup wait time, which is programmed for
512 switching cycles, the device shuts down and restarts after the hiccup time of 16384 cycles. Hiccup mode
helps reduce the device power dissipation under severe overcurrent conditions.
The low-side MOSFET can also sink current from load. If the low-side sinking current limit is exceeded, the
low-side MOSFET is turned off immediately for the rest of that clock cycle. In this scenario, both MOSFETs are
off until the start of the next cycle.
```

##### 6.3.12 Output Overvoltage Protection (OVP)

```text
The TPS54308 incorporates an overvoltage transient protection (OVTP) circuit to minimize output voltage
overshoot when recovering from output fault conditions or strong unload transients. The OVTP circuit includes
an overvoltage comparator to compare the FB pin voltage and internal thresholds. When the FB pin voltage goes
above 118% × Vref, the high-side MOSFET is forced off. When the FB pin voltage falls below 104% × Vref, the
high-side MOSFET is enabled again.
```

##### 6.3.13 Thermal Shutdown

```text
The internal thermal-shutdown circuitry forces the device to stop switching if the junction temperature exceeds
165°C typically. When the junction temperature drops below 155°C typically, the internal thermal-hiccup timer
begins to count. The device reinitiates the power-up sequence after the built-in thermal-shutdown hiccup time
(32768 cycles) is over.
```

###### 7.2.3.6.2 Output Capacitor Selection

```text
Consider three primary factors when selecting the value of the output capacitor. The output capacitor determines
the modulator pole, the output voltage ripple, and how the regulator responds to a large change in load current.
The output capacitance must be selected based on the more stringent of these three criteria.
The desired response to a large change in the load current is the first criterion. The output capacitor must supply
the load with current when the regulator cannot. This situation occurs if the desired hold-up times are present for
the regulator. In this case, the output capacitor must hold the output voltage above a certain level for a specified
amount of time after the input power is removed. The regulator is also temporarily unable to supply sufficient
output current if a large, fast increase occurs affecting the current requirements of the load, such as a transition
```

#### 7.3 Power Supply Recommendations

```text
The device is designed to operate from an input voltage supply range between 4.5V and 28V. This input supply
must be well regulated. If the input supply is located more than a few inches from the device or converter,
additional bulk capacitance can be required in addition to the ceramic bypass capacitors. An electrolytic
capacitor with a value of 47 µF is a typical choice.
```

#### 7.4 Layout

##### 7.4.1 Layout Guidelines

```text
•  VIN and GND traces must as wide as possible to reduce trace impedance. The wide areas are also of
advantage from the view point of heat dissipation.
•  The input capacitor and output capacitor must placed as close to the device as possible to minimize trace
impedance.
•  Provide sufficient vias for the input capacitor and output capacitor.
•  Keep the SW trace as physically short and wide as practical to minimize radiated emissions.
•  Do not allow switching current to flow under the device.
•  A separate VOUT path must connected to the upper feedback resistor.
•  Make a Kelvin connection to the GND pin for the feedback path.
•  Voltage feedback loop must placed away from the high-voltage switching trace, and preferably has ground
shield.
•  The trace of the VFB node must as small as possible to avoid noise coupling.
•  The GND trace between the output capacitor and the GND pin must as wide as possible to minimize the
trace impedance.
```
